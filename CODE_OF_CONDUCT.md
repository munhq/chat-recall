# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code, not
about the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Give feedback on the work: what is wrong, why it matters, what you would do.
- Accept that other people have different constraints — a different stack, less
  time, a machine that behaves differently from yours.
- Take a correction well. Everyone here has shipped a bug this month.
- Ask before assuming bad intent. Most rudeness in issue trackers is a language
  gap or a bad day, not malice.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or comments about someone's identity, background,
  or ability rather than their code.
- Harassment of any kind, in the open or in private messages that started here.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled arguments, brigading a thread, or
  demanding free labour from maintainers.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, commit messages,
and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Mail **contact@chatrecall.dev**. Reports go to the maintainers and are not
shared further without your agreement, unless we are legally required to.

Say what happened and where. A link to the thread is usually enough. You do not
need to prove anything or write it up formally — a two-line report is fine.

## What happens next

A maintainer will read it and reply. Depending on what happened, the response
ranges from a private word, to editing or removing content, to a temporary or
permanent ban from the project.

Maintainers who break these rules face the same consequences as anyone else,
and more of them — the project is small enough that a maintainer behaving badly
is the version of this that actually does damage.
